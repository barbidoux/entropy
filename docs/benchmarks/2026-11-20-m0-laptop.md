## Memory bandwidth (#5)

512 MiB buffers, 8 times the 64 MiB L2, so these are DRAM figures. 1 GB = 10⁹ bytes.
Peak 576 GB/s at 9001 MHz (18 Gb/s × 256 bits), 512 GB/s at 8001 MHz. This run was at
9001 MHz.

| Pass | GB/s (median of 50) | Share of the 576 GB/s peak |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Copy, `float` per thread | 503 | 87 % |
| Copy, `vec4` per thread | 502 | 87 % |
| `wp.copy` (driver memcpy) | 498 | 86 % |
| Read only, `vec4` (zero-filled buffer) | 560 | 97 % |

**Planning figure: 450 GB/s** for read-write streaming in the sustained state (the
503 GB/s copy scaled to the 8001 MHz memory clock seen under the power cap). A kernel
moving B bytes per cell over N cells costs at least B·N / 450 GB/s.

## Jacobi stencil (#6)

One sweep of `entropy.linalg.jacobi` (7-point stencil, float32), timed inside a CUDA
graph of 100 sweeps. The kernel marches 8 cells per thread along i, adopted during the
review of #22: the reviewer timed it unofficially (about 20 s warm-up, power mode not
checked, both kernels in one process) at 27.3 µs against 66.8 µs per sweep at 128³ and
188 µs against 318 µs at 192³ for one cell per thread, 1.7–2.4× faster. The
streaming pass moves the same 12 bytes per cell with no stencil; it is a reference, not
a bound (at 128³ the stencil beat it).

| Grid | Cells | ms per sweep (graph) | Effective GB/s | vs streaming | Sweeps in 6 ms | ps per cell |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 128³ | 2.10 M | 0.0282 | 892 (L2-resident) | 109 % | 213 | 13.5 |
| 160 × 128 × 96 (house, 6.25 cm) | 1.97 M | 0.0272 | 868 (L2-resident) | 104 % | 221 | 13.8 |
| 192³ | 7.08 M | 0.199 (single launch 0.160) | 426 | 92 % | 30 | 28.2 |

- At 128³ the three arrays (24 MiB) fit in the 64 MiB L2 cache. At 192³ (81 MiB) they do
  not, and the cost per cell doubles.
- A real solver keeps many more fields alive (a gas step about 15 arrays: 120 MiB at
  128³), so it will not be L2-resident. **The DRAM rate, 28 ps per cell per sweep, is the
  planning rate**; the L2 rate, 13.5 ps, is the optimistic bound.
- At 192³ the single launch (0.160 ms) and the graph (0.199 ms) differ by 25 %. The
  memory clock dropped from 9001 to 8001 MHz during that benchmark (11 % less peak) and
  the SM clock from 1665 to 1335 MHz; telemetry samples cannot separate the two.

### Pressure solve and gas step estimates

A real pressure solve is multigrid-preconditioned CG (MGPCG, SPEC 5.2), measured in M1.
These are **estimates** from the sweep cost, with this model:

- One MGPCG iteration ≈ 12 fine-sweep equivalents of memory traffic (V-cycle: 4
  smoothing sweeps, residual, restriction and prolongation, about 1/7 more for coarser
  levels; CG: one matrix-vector product, two dot products, three vector updates), and
  about 50 kernels. Assumed: a lean V-cycle (2 + 2 weighted-Jacobi sweeps, about 5
  levels down to 8³, a coarsest solve of at most 10 kernels), float32, unfused kernels
  and a constant-coefficient operator. With the variational solid weights of SPEC 5.2
  each operator application also reads 3 face weights: about 18 sweep equivalents per
  iteration, so read the solve figures as 12–18. McAdams et al. (2010) use a heavier
  V-cycle (boundary Gauss–Seidel with more sweeps near walls, an iterated coarsest
  solve): about 250 kernels per V-cycle, so about 3,000 per solve and 3.6 ms of kernel
  overhead instead of 0.7 ms.
- 12 iterations per solve, from a cold start to a 10⁻⁵ residual: **144 sweep equivalents
  and about 600 kernels per solve**. Each kernel adds about 1.2 µs of GPU time (measured
  below), so 0.7 ms per solve whatever the grid; this also stands for the coarse levels,
  which are bound by latency, not bandwidth.
- A gas step adds advection of about ten fields with error correction (≈ 60 sweep
  equivalents) and combustion, buoyancy and vorticity confinement (≈ 10): **about 215 sweep
  equivalents and about 650 kernels**.

Costs **per substep**, DRAM rate (planning) and L2 rate (optimistic), kernel overhead
included, with the lean V-cycle and constant coefficients (12 sweep equivalents per
iteration):

| Grid | Pressure solve, planning | Pressure solve, optimistic | Gas step, planning | Gas step, optimistic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 96³ (0.88 M cells) | 4.3 ms | 2.4 ms | 6.1 ms | 3.3 ms |
| House liquid grid (1.97 M) | 8.7 ms | 4.6 ms | — | — |
| 128³ (2.10 M) | 9.2 ms | 4.8 ms | 13.5 ms | 6.8 ms |
| 192³ (7.08 M) | 29 ms | — | 44 ms | — |

**Substeps.** SPEC 5.9 splits each frame into substeps by the CFL condition, up to 4. A
fire plume rising at about 5 m/s in 7.5 cm cells crosses 2.2 cells in 1/30 s, so it
needs 3 substeps per frame at 30 fps. Gas costs above multiply by the substep count.

Consequences:

- **Gas:** neither 128³ (6.8–13.5 ms) nor even 96³ with several substeps fits the 6 ms
  budget. 192³ is out of reach. The gas box size, the substep rule for gas
  (semi-Lagrangian advection is stable beyond one cell per substep) and the pressure
  solve frequency are open until M3 measures the real solver.
- **Liquid:** the pressure solve on the full house grid (4.6–8.7 ms per substep) could take
  the whole 9 ms liquid budget before any particle transfer; water at 2 m/s in 6.25 cm
  cells needs 2 substeps at 30 fps, so 9–17 ms per frame. It is solved only on liquid cells,
  which are a fraction of the house in most scenes; M1 measures it.

## Kernel launch overhead (#7)

A 256-thread kernel that does almost nothing, so costs are overheads. Host cost is
wall time on the Python thread from an idle GPU; GPU cost is stream time between CUDA
events divided by the kernels.

| Path | Host µs per kernel (median, p10–p90) | GPU µs per kernel | Kernels in 2 ms of host time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `wp.launch` from Python | 14.6 (12.7–15.5) | 15.9 (GPU waits for Python) | 137 |
| Recorded command (`record_cmd`) | 9.0 (7.9–10.0) | 8.5 | 222 |
| CUDA graph of 10 kernels | 0.48 (0.41–3.1) | 1.17 | 4,200 |
| CUDA graph of 100 kernels | 0.20 (0.19–0.28) | 1.27 | 9,900 |
| CUDA graph of 1,000 kernels | 0.69 (0.31–1.10) | 1.09 | 2,900 (1,800 at p90) |

- Plain launches cannot carry a solver frame: 137 kernels use up the 2 ms budget.
- Graphs cut host cost 20–70× per kernel. One graph launch is not linear in its size:
  graphs of about 100 kernels were cheapest per kernel; large ones were slower and noisy.
- Two limits, not one (both measured on tiny 256-thread kernels; for real kernels they are
  estimates):
  - **Host:** about 1,800 kernels launched from graphs per frame at the p90 cost.
    Conditional graph nodes (`wp.is_conditional_graph_supported()` returned true on the
    laptop; observed by hand, not in the JSON) run solver iteration loops on the GPU, so
    host launches do not grow with iterations.
  - **GPU:** every kernel executed costs about 1.2 µs of GPU time on top of its work,
    including iterations inside conditional nodes. 2,000 executed kernels per frame cost
    2.4 ms of the 22 ms simulation budget.
- Host enqueuing overlaps the GPU's work on earlier kernels, except at synchronisation
  points: CUDA–OpenGL map and unmap each frame, timing and data read-backs, and OpenGL
  and ImGui calls on the same thread. This is reasoning, not a measurement; the frame
  loop measures it from M1.

**ADR 0002 check.** Its falsification criterion is orchestration above 2 ms per frame once
substeps are graphs. Verdict: **holds if solver iteration loops are conditional graph
nodes**. Without them, two MGPCG solvers (about 600 kernels each) at 4 substeps launch
about 4,800 kernels, above the host limit. With them, the binding constraint becomes
the GPU overhead per executed kernel, which counts against the simulation budget.

**Default frame target (SPEC 13):** ADR 0008. One simulation advance per frame of the
frame time × the time scale, clamped to 1/30 s, frame rate capped at 60 fps; solvers
substep by CFL inside that advance. SPEC 5.9 is reworded to say so explicitly.

## Method changes for the next session (S7 of the review of #24)

- Before each benchmark, a short streaming load (10 s) restores the sustained state, so a
  benchmark that barely loads the GPU does not let the next one start cool.
- The read-only bandwidth pass reads a non-zero buffer, ruling out any compression
  effect on zero data.
- Still open (issue #25): interleaving the Jacobi and streaming repeats, and sampling
  telemetry during each benchmark instead of before and after.

## Physics audit (M0 gate)

The milestone's physics auditor re-derived every figure above from the raw results and
the references and found them consistent. Its corrections are applied: the substep
count multiplies every per-substep cost, a 0.5–1 MW furniture fire rises at about
7 m/s (McCaffrey 1979) and reaches the 4-substep cap in 7.5 cm cells, the MGPCG
assumptions and range are stated, and estimates are labelled. ADR 0013 records the
time-stepping consequences.
