After warm-up the GPU is both hot and at its power limit, and its SM clock drops from
2.3 GHz to about 1.3–1.7 GHz. That is the sustained state SPEC 3 asks to budget for.
Memory-bound kernels hardly notice; compute-bound kernels (M1 particle transfers) will.

## Memory bandwidth (#5)

512 MiB buffers, 8 times the 64 MiB L2, so these are DRAM figures. 1 GB = 10⁹ bytes.
Theoretical peak 576 GB/s (18 Gb/s × 256 bits; the reported memory clock gives the
same).

| Pass | GB/s (median of 50) | Share of peak |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Copy, `float` per thread | 503 | 87 % |
| Copy, `vec4` per thread | 502 | 87 % |
| `wp.copy` (driver memcpy) | 498 | 86 % |
| Read only, `vec4` | 560 | 97 % |

**Planning figure: 500 GB/s** for read-write streaming. A kernel moving B bytes per cell
over N cells costs at least B·N / 500 GB/s.

## Jacobi stencil (#6)

One sweep of `entropy.linalg.jacobi` (7-point stencil, float32), timed inside a CUDA
graph of 100 sweeps. The kernel marches 8 cells per thread along i (adopted from the
review of #22, about 2× faster than one cell per thread). "Streaming" is a pass with
the same 12 bytes per cell and no stencil: the ceiling for this kernel.

| Grid | Cells | ms per sweep | Effective GB/s | vs streaming | Sweeps in 6 ms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 128³ | 2.10 M | 0.028 | 892 (L2-resident) | 109 % | 213 |
| 160 × 128 × 96 (house, 6.25 cm) | 1.97 M | 0.027 | 868 (L2-resident) | 104 % | 221 |
| 192³ | 7.08 M | 0.199 (0.160 single) | 426 | 92 % | 30 |

- At 128³ the three arrays (24 MiB) fit in the 64 MiB L2 cache, so a sweep runs faster
  than DRAM allows. At 192³ (81 MiB) they do not, and the cost jumps 7× for 3.4× the
  cells. A real gas step has many more fields alive than these three, so L2 residency
  will be partial; budgets below use the 192³ rate per cell (28 ps) as the safe figure
  and the 128³ rate (13 ps) as the optimistic one.
- The spread between single launch, batched and graph at 192³ (0.16–0.20 ms) reflects
  clock changes while throttled.

### Pressure solve estimate

A real pressure solve is multigrid-preconditioned CG (MGPCG, SPEC 5.2), measured in M1.
Estimate from these sweeps, stated as such:

- One MGPCG iteration ≈ 12 fine-sweep equivalents (V-cycle: 4 smoothing sweeps,
  residual, restriction and prolongation, plus about 1/7 for coarser levels; CG: one
  matrix-vector product, two dot products, three vector updates).
- 10–15 iterations from a cold start to a 10⁻⁵ residual; fewer when warm-started from
  the previous frame. Take 12 → about **150 sweep equivalents per solve**.

| Grid | Pressure solve, optimistic (L2) | Pressure solve, safe (DRAM) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 128³ | 4.2 ms | 8.8 ms |
| House liquid grid, 2.0 M cells | 4.0 ms | 8.3 ms |
| 192³ | — | 30 ms |

A gas step also advects about ten fields with error correction (≈ 60 sweep
equivalents) and runs combustion, buoyancy and vorticity confinement (≈ 10), so a full
gas step is about 220 sweep equivalents: **≈ 6 ms at 128³ (L2-friendly) up to 13 ms;
≈ 44 ms at 192³**.

## Kernel launch overhead (#7)

A 256-thread kernel that does almost nothing, so costs are overheads.

| Path | Host µs per kernel | GPU µs per kernel | Kernels in 2 ms of host time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `wp.launch` from Python | 14.6 (p10–p90 12.7–15.5) | 15.9 (GPU waits for Python) | 137 |
| Recorded command (`record_cmd`) | 9.0 | 8.5 | 222 |
| CUDA graph of 10 kernels | 0.48 | 1.17 | 4,200 |
| CUDA graph of 100 kernels | 0.20 | 1.27 | 9,900 |
| CUDA graph of 1,000 kernels | 0.69 (p10–p90 0.31–1.10) | 1.09 | 2,900 |

- Plain launches are hopeless for a solver frame: 137 kernels exhaust the 2 ms budget.
- Graphs cut host cost 20–70× per kernel. Launching one graph is not linear in its size:
  graphs of about 100 kernels were cheapest per kernel, very large ones were slower and
  noisy.
- Even inside a graph each kernel costs about **1.2 µs of GPU time** on top of its work.
  2,000 kernels per frame would spend 2.4 ms of the GPU budget on overhead alone.
- Host enqueuing runs while the GPU works on earlier kernels. It costs frame time only
  when it exceeds the GPU time or when Python waits for the GPU (read-backs).
- Warp reports conditional graph nodes as supported here
  (`wp.is_conditional_graph_supported()`), so solver iteration loops can run on the GPU
  without the host launching each iteration.

**ADR 0002 check.** Its falsification criterion is orchestration above 2 ms per frame once
substeps are graphs. Measured: not falsified as long as a frame launches fewer than
about 2,900 kernels from graphs (worst graph size). An MGPCG solve is several hundred
to a thousand kernels, so two solvers at the 4-substep cap would cross the line. The
mitigations are planned (conditional nodes for iteration loops, fused kernels, graph
size around 100); M1 measures the real liquid frame against it.

## What changes in SPEC section 3

| Item | Before | After | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gas, smoke and fire capacity | 128³ to 192³ cells in 6 ms | **128³ cells in 6 ms**; 192³ is out of reach (≈ 44 ms per step) | 192³ no longer fits in L2 and costs 7× a 128³ sweep; a full gas step at 128³ is already ≈ 6 ms |
| Liquid | 1–2 M particles, ≤ 2 M active cells, 9 ms | Kept; the pressure solve on the full house grid is 4–8 ms by estimate, so particle transfers get what is left; M1 measures | Sweep cost on the house grid measured; particle transfers are compute-bound and not measured yet |
| Kernel launches | — | **Solver frames launch from CUDA graphs only; at most about 2,000 kernels per frame**; iterative loops use conditional graph nodes | 14.6 µs per plain launch; 0.2–0.7 µs host and 1.2 µs GPU per kernel in a graph |
| Planning bandwidth | — | **500 GB/s** read-write streaming, after warm-up | Measured, 87 % of peak, unchanged by throttling |
| Sustained GPU state | — | 83–89 °C, 1.3–1.7 GHz, 110 W power limit, thermally and power capped | Telemetry |

Frame budget at 30 fps, VRAM and RAM budgets: unchanged.

**Default frame target (SPEC 13):** decided in ADR 0008. One GPU runs simulation and
rendering in sequence, so a 22 ms simulation step and a 60 fps render rate cannot both
hold. The frame targets 60 fps when the scene allows and falls back to 30 fps under
load, simulation and rendering together, as SPEC 5.9 already describes.
