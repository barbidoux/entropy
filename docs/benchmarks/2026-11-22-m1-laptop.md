## The gate (#57): 1 M particles at 30 fps or more

`tests/perf/test_m1_gate.py` (ADR 0029): the game itself (Test Tank, liquid, every
pass, HUD, 60 fps cap, vsync) with 1,048,576 particles, 600 frames per part, the first
60 not counted. Frame rate from each frame's real time; p10 is the rate held in 90 % of
the frames. GPU times from the game's own timers (medians).

Two official runs:

| Run | GPU during the run | Dam break: median, p10 | Hose: median, p10 | Hose frames under 30 fps | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 12:22 session, first gate test | SM 2235–2310 MHz (power cap 46 % of samples), 80–87 °C | 60.0, 58.7 fps | 39.4, 35.0 fps | 1 of 540 | pass |
| 13:37, after #80's review, after about 80 min of near-continuous GPU load | SM 1665–2310, median 2010 MHz (thermal slowdown 79 % of samples), 82–87 °C | 60.0, 58.7 fps | 33.4, **29.8 fps** | 64 of 540 | **fail** |

- The dam break is far from the limit in both. **The hose part is at the limit**: it
  is GPU-bound near 30 ms a frame, so it moves with the GPU's clocks.
- The first run's test lacked the review's checks (no freeze, the hose's flow
  accounted for), but its scenario and view were the same. A run at 13:19 is set aside:
  capturing the mouse had turned its camera (the test now pins it).
- The second run's checks hold: no freeze; the hose released 9,851 particles over 15.0 s
  of simulated time, 9,808 in the tank and 42 in flight.

| Second run (13:37) | Dam break | Hose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| World speed (simulated over real time) | 1.000 | 0.819 |
| Substeps, frames at 1 / 2 / 4 | 373 / 167 / 0 | 0 / 0 / 540 |
| Overruns, unconverged solves | 3, 0 | 212, 0 |
| GPU, simulation stage's span (ms, median; p90) | 6.68; 12.51 | 27.75; 31.31 |
| GPU, liquid pressure | 3.37 | 12.06 |
| GPU, liquid transfers | 1.69 | 9.86 |
| GPU, liquid motion | 0.11 | 0.49 |
| GPU, liquid pass (OpenGL) | 0.58 | 3.87 |
| GPU, scene, glass, tone map, HUD | 0.06 | 0.08 |

- **The simulation stage's span** is the GPU time between the frame's simulation
  events: the tools, the liquid's whole step (sort, labels, volume, extrapolation and
  its phases) and the checkpoint copy every 10 s, and any time the GPU waits for the CPU
  to enqueue.
- **Stage times overlap.** The simulation (CUDA) and the passes (OpenGL) share the GPU.
  In the hose part the transfers read 2.5 ms per substep and the liquid pass 3.9 ms,
  two to three times their cost measured alone (1.16 ms, and 1.16–1.69 ms
  for the nearest views, below). They show where
  time goes, not costs to add up.
- At one substep the liquid fits its 9 ms budget (SPEC 3) in the median frame; a third
  of the dam break's frames take two substeps (p90 12.5 ms). Under the hose's 10 m/s jet
  every frame runs at the 4-substep cap: the simulation's span (about 23–28 ms over the
  runs) exceeds SPEC 3's whole 22 ms simulation budget, and the world runs slower than
  real time (0.82× here; 212 frames needed more substeps than the cap). 30 fps held,
  when it held, because rendering is light and M1 runs no other solver.
- First run, for comparison: simulation span 6.82 and 23.09 ms, pressure 3.37 and 9.80,
  transfers 1.69 and 7.66, liquid pass 0.58 and 1.79 ms. In the hose part that is
  about 5.8 ms per substep: pressure 2.4, transfers 1.9, the rest 1.4.

## Particle transfers (#65, ADR 0025)

`tests/perf/test_m1_transfers.py`, 1,048,576 particles on the Test Tank's grid, per
substep:

| Order | Particles to grid | Grid to particles | Both |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Random | 3.73 ms | 0.95 ms | 4.67 ms |
| The bucket's layers | 3.60 ms | 0.17 ms | 3.77 ms |
| `fill_box`'s lattice (already half-cell order) | 1.04 ms | 0.13 ms | 1.17 ms |
| Sorted by half-cell, as the liquid keeps them | 1.03 ms | 0.13 ms | 1.16 ms |

The sort costs 0.86 ms on shuffled particles and 0.47 ms on the previous frame's order,
0.25 ms a frame net of the copy it replaces. #65's threshold of about 2 ms of particles
to grid per substep is met without a shared-memory version.

## Pressure solve (ADR 0016)

`tests/perf/test_m1_pressure_solve.py`: MGPCG from zero after a relabel, 12 iterations
to a relative residual of 10⁻⁵ or less.

| Grid | Levels | Per substep | Per frame at 2 substeps |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Test Tank, 72 × 72 × 40 | 4 | 4.41 ms | 8.82 ms |
| House, 160 × 128 × 96 | 5 | 17.96 ms | 35.93 ms |
| 128³ | 6 | 19.97 ms | 39.95 ms |

In play the solve starts from the last pressure: the gate's dam break took 8 iterations
(median) and 3.4 ms a frame. The house grid's solve, from zero, is 2.1 to 3.6 times
SPEC 3's estimate of 5.0–8.7 ms per substep, and 1.3 times the unofficial 13.8 ms of
#50. It is the liquid's main risk for the house (M6).

## Frame pacing (ADR 0026)

`tests/perf/test_m1_frame_pacing.py`, with vsync on the 60 Hz display:

| Work per frame | Longer frame | Median before / after | Longest run over 25 ms after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 ms CPU / GPU | 25 ms | 16.70 / 16.66, 16.65 / 16.66 ms | 0, 0 |
| 15 ms CPU / GPU | 40 ms | 16.71 / 16.72, 16.79 / 16.88 ms | 0, 1 |
| 17.5 ms CPU / GPU | 25 ms | 17.57 / 17.56, 20.22 / 20.23 ms | 0, 1 |

The loop never stays a refresh behind. With 17.9 ms of GPU work measured, a frame
takes 20.2 ms (18.5 ms unofficially in ADR 0026); the cause of the extra 2.3 ms is not
measured. With 15 ms of GPU work, 9 isolated frames after the long one went over 25 ms
(ADR 0026's unofficial run had none); none were consecutive.

## Conditional graph nodes (ADR 0026)

40 iterations on 96³: batches with read-backs 2.26 ms, one conditional node 1.93 ms,
unrolled 1.74 ms; the node costs 4.7 µs per iteration and its enqueue 68 µs. As in
ADR 0026, the pressure solve keeps its batches.

## M0 benchmarks, again

Bandwidth, launch overhead, stencils and interop ran again under the same conditions:
stencil 128³ 0.029 ms per sweep (graph), house grid 0.030 ms, 192³ 0.158 ms; interop at
1 M particles 0.20 ms zero-copy against 5.80 ms copied through the host. Since
telemetry during each run (#25), stencil results are compared with each other, not
with the M0 reports (`README.md`); interop is 11.5 % faster than M0's 0.23 ms. Nothing in
SPEC 3 changes for them.

## What changes in SPEC section 3

- The liquid's row: the measured cost per frame at 1 M particles (the simulation's
  span: 6.7–6.8 ms at one substep, 23–28 ms at four), the transfers sorted by half-cell
  (1.16 ms per substep plus 0.25 ms of sorting per frame), and the pressure solve from
  zero (4.4 ms per substep on the Test Tank grid, 18.0 ms on the house grid).
- At risk, named: the liquid's 9 ms holds at one substep only. Fast water (the hose at
  10 m/s) needs four, which takes more than the whole 22 ms simulation budget and puts
  the hose part of the gate at 30 fps exactly, pass or fail with the GPU's thermal
  state. M3 (gas) and M6 (the house) need it lower: fewer substeps for fast but sparse
  water, or a cheaper substep.
- The gas row: the M1 pressure solver from zero at 128³ measured 20.0 ms per substep
  officially, against 14.7 ms unofficially (ADR 0016).
