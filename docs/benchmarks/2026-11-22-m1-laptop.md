## The gate (#57): 1 M particles at 30 fps or more

`tests/perf/test_m1_gate.py`: the game itself (Test Tank, liquid, every pass, HUD,
60 fps cap, vsync) with 1,048,576 particles, 600 frames per part, the first 60 not
counted. Frame rate from each frame's real time; GPU times from the game's own timers
(medians).

| Part | Frame rate, median | Frame rate held 90 % of the time (p10) | Frames under 30 fps | Substeps | Pressure iterations (median, max) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dam break (a step: 2.375 m against 1.625 m) | 60.0 fps | 58.7 fps | 0 of 540 | 1 | 8, 12 |
| Hose into the moving water (20 L/s at 10 m/s) | 39.4 fps | 35.0 fps | 1 of 540 | 4 | 8, 12 |

**The gate passes:** 30 fps or more in both parts, held 90 % of the time.

| GPU time per frame (ms, median) | Dam break | Hose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Liquid: pressure | 3.37 | 9.80 |
| Liquid: transfers | 1.69 | 7.66 |
| Liquid: motion | 0.11 | 0.43 |
| Liquid: whole step (all phases, sort, labels, volume, extrapolation) | 6.82 | 23.09 |
| Render: liquid pass | 0.58 | 1.79 |
| Render: scene, glass, tone map, HUD | 0.06 | 0.08 |

- At one substep the liquid takes 6.8 ms, within its 9 ms budget (SPEC 3). Under the
  hose's 10 m/s jet it takes 4 substeps (the CFL cap, ADR 0013) and 23.1 ms, 2.6 times
  the budget. The game still holds 30 fps because M1 runs no other solver: the 22 ms
  simulation budget is the liquid's alone.
- In the hose part the liquid costs about 5.8 ms per substep at 1 M particles:
  pressure 2.4 ms, transfers 1.9 ms, the rest 1.5 ms.

## Particle transfers (#65, ADR 0025)

`tests/perf/test_m1_transfers.py`, 1,048,576 particles on the Test Tank's grid, per
substep:

| Order | Particles to grid | Grid to particles | Both |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Random | 3.73 ms | 0.95 ms | 4.67 ms |
| The bucket's layers | 3.60 ms | 0.17 ms | 3.77 ms |
| `fill_box`'s lattice (already half-cell order) | 1.04 ms | 0.13 ms | 1.17 ms |
| Sorted by half-cell, as the liquid keeps them | 1.03 ms | 0.13 ms | 1.16 ms |

The sort costs 0.86 ms on shuffled particles and 0.47 ms on the previous frame's order,
0.25 ms a frame net of the copy it replaces. #65's threshold of about 2 ms of particles
to grid per substep is met without a shared-memory version.

## Pressure solve (ADR 0016)

`tests/perf/test_m1_pressure_solve.py`: MGPCG from zero after a relabel, 12 iterations
to a relative residual of 10⁻⁵ or less.

| Grid | Levels | Per substep | Per frame at 2 substeps |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Test Tank, 72 × 72 × 40 | 4 | 4.41 ms | 8.82 ms |
| House, 160 × 128 × 96 | 5 | 17.96 ms | 35.93 ms |
| 128³ | 6 | 19.97 ms | 39.95 ms |

In play the solve starts from the last pressure: the gate's dam break took 8 iterations
(median) and 3.4 ms a frame. The house grid's solve, from zero, is 2.1 to 3.6 times
SPEC 3's estimate of 5.0–8.7 ms per substep, and 1.3 times the unofficial 13.8 ms of
#50. It is the liquid's main risk for the house (M6).

## Frame pacing (ADR 0026)

`tests/perf/test_m1_frame_pacing.py`, with vsync on the 60 Hz display:

| Work per frame | Longer frame | Median before / after | Longest run over 25 ms after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 ms CPU / GPU | 25 ms | 16.70 / 16.66, 16.65 / 16.66 ms | 0, 0 |
| 15 ms CPU / GPU | 40 ms | 16.71 / 16.72, 16.79 / 16.88 ms | 0, 1 |
| 17.5 ms CPU / GPU | 25 ms | 17.57 / 17.56, 20.22 / 20.23 ms | 0, 1 |

The loop never stays a refresh behind. With 17.5 ms of GPU work after the warm-up, a
frame takes 20.2 ms (18.5 ms unofficially in ADR 0026, cooler): the GPU work itself is
slower when hot.

## Conditional graph nodes (ADR 0026)

40 iterations on 96³: batches with read-backs 2.26 ms, one conditional node 1.93 ms,
unrolled 1.74 ms; the node costs 4.7 µs per iteration and its enqueue 68 µs. As in
ADR 0026, the pressure solve keeps its batches.

## M0 benchmarks, again

Bandwidth, launch overhead, stencils and interop ran again under the same conditions:
stencil 128³ 0.029 ms per sweep (graph), house grid 0.030 ms, 192³ 0.158 ms; interop at
1 M particles 0.20 ms zero-copy against 5.80 ms copied through the host. M0's two
sessions read 0.028–0.032, 0.027–0.029 and 0.185–0.199 ms for the three stencils, and
0.23 ms for interop. 128³ is within that spread, the house grid 3 % above it, 192³
15 % and interop 13 % faster. Nothing in SPEC 3 changes for them.

## What changes in SPEC section 3

- The liquid's row: the measured cost per frame at 1 M particles (6.8 ms at one
  substep, 23.1 ms at four), the transfers sorted by half-cell (1.16 ms per substep
  plus 0.25 ms of sorting per frame), and the pressure solve from zero (4.4 ms per
  substep on the Test Tank grid, 18.0 ms on the house grid).
- At risk, named: the liquid's 9 ms holds at one substep only. Fast water (the hose at
  10 m/s) needs four, and the frame then holds 30 fps only because no other solver
  shares the GPU. M3 (gas) and M6 (the house) must bring it down: fewer substeps for
  fast but sparse water, or a cheaper substep.
